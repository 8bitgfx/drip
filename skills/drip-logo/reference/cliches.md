# Clichés, conventions and amateur tells

Every category has a visual default. Some of it is useful (people need to know what you are), most of it is
camouflage. For each category: what works, what to honour, what to break for positioning, and the clichés that
make a mark invisible. Use it to cut concepts, and to brief the parallel concept agents.

Rule: **honour the convention that tells people what you are; break the one that makes you look like everyone
else.** Break one convention, on purpose, with a sentence on why. Breaking all of them reads as wrong category.

## By category

### Tech, SaaS, developer tools
- **Clichés**: `</>` brackets, `>_` prompts, cubes and isometric boxes, hexagons, nodes-and-lines networks,
  infinity loops, rocket ships, lightning bolts, a rounded-square gradient app icon, lowercase geometric sans.
- **Honour**: works as a 16px favicon and a GitHub avatar; one colour; crisp at small sizes.
- **Break**: the geometric-sans-lowercase wordmark (try a serif, a mono with character, a drawn letter); the
  blue/purple palette.

### AI products
- **Clichés**: sparkle ✦ or four-point stars, brains, neural nodes, orbs with gradients, spirals, eyes, swirling
  "flower" knots, purple-to-blue gradients, the letters "AI" in the mark.
- **Honour**: calm and trustworthy beats magical; a clear silhouette.
- **Break**: the magic metaphor. Find what the product *does* (sorts, writes, checks) and draw that behaviour.

### Fintech and banking
- **Clichés**: upward arrows, bar charts, shields, lions and eagles, coins, pillars, globes, "trust blue".
- **Honour**: stability, legibility, restraint; works on a card and in a 32px app icon.
- **Break**: the blue (deep green, black, a warm colour with conviction); the corporate serif (a precise grotesk).

### Crypto and web3
- **Clichés**: coins with a letter, hexagon chains, blocks, rockets, moons, neon gradients, glitch effects.
- **Honour**: a symbol that works as a token icon in a circle.
- **Break**: the neon-on-black costume; draw something that would also look credible on a bank statement.

### Law, consulting, professional services
- **Clichés**: scales of justice, pillars, gavels, Lady Justice, laurel wreaths, initials in a serif inside a
  circle or shield, "&" between surnames as the only idea.
- **Honour**: seriousness, legibility of the partners' names.
- **Break**: the stock serif; a confident grotesk wordmark with one custom ampersand can still read as serious.

### Health and medical
- **Clichés**: crosses, hearts with a pulse line, caduceus, hands holding a heart, leaves in hands, stethoscopes.
- **Honour**: calm, clean, accessible colours; avoid alarm reds.
- **Break**: the clinical blue-and-green; warmth and plain language read as more human.

### Wellness and beauty
- **Clichés**: lotus, leaves, a woman's face profile, droplets, circles with a gradient, thin script, beige.
- **Honour**: tactility, a premium feel.
- **Break**: the thin script and the beige; a heavy confident wordmark stands out in a sea of whispers.

### Food and drink, cafés
- **Clichés**: coffee beans, cups with steam, chef hats, forks and spoons, wheat stalks, a mascot chef, chalkboard
  script.
- **Honour**: appetite (warm colours work), works on cups, bags and signs, one colour for stamps.
- **Break**: the illustrated object; a strong wordmark or a shape from the place (the shopfront, the street).

### Real estate and architecture
- **Clichés**: roof chevrons over a letter, house outlines, keys, skylines, a door.
- **Honour**: stability; signage legibility.
- **Break**: the roof; a construction-grid wordmark says "built well" without a house.

### Eco, energy, sustainability
- **Clichés**: leaves, globes, recycling arrows, hands cupping the earth, sun rays, wind turbines, green-to-blue.
- **Honour**: honesty; no greenwashing tropes.
- **Break**: the leaf-green; a mark about the system (cycles, grids, flows) reads more credible.

### Education and kids
- **Clichés**: graduation caps, open books, owls, apples, pencils, ABC blocks, rainbow colours, bubbly fonts.
- **Honour**: friendliness, legibility for young readers, works on small screens.
- **Break**: the rainbow; one bold colour and a character with one strong idea.

### Games and studios
- **Clichés**: controllers, pixel swords, shields with crossed weapons, skulls, dragons, flames, chrome bevels,
  glitch text, "esports" angular mascots.
- **Honour**: works as a splash screen, a store icon (square, often tiny) and in motion; strong silhouette.
- **Break**: the bevel and chrome; a flat, graphic mark with one strong shape outlasts trend effects.

### Music, media, podcasts
- **Clichés**: notes, headphones, waveforms, play buttons, vinyl records, microphones, equalizer bars.
- **Honour**: works as a square cover avatar at 60px.
- **Break**: the audio icon; type-led marks from record sleeves and posters.

### Creative tools and design
- **Clichés**: pen nibs, brushes, paint drops and drips, overlapping colour circles, pencils, rainbows, the
  Bézier handle icon.
- **Honour**: craft (the mark is a demo of the product's quality bar).
- **Break**: the paint drop. Drip learned this the hard way: a glossy red drop read as blood. If liquid is the
  idea, it must be one crafted path with an idea behind it (paint escaping the selection box), never the drop
  itself.

### Travel and mobility
- **Clichés**: planes with trails, globes, compasses, map pins, suitcases, swooshes, wings.
- **Honour**: motion and clarity; works on vehicles and uniforms.
- **Break**: the swoosh; a typographic mark with a sense of direction.

### Pets and animals
- **Clichés**: paw prints, dog and cat silhouettes, bones, hearts with paws.
- **Honour**: warmth.
- **Break**: the paw print; character or a wordmark with one animal-derived letter.

### Security and privacy
- **Clichés**: shields, padlocks, keyholes, eyes, fingerprints, checkmarks in shields.
- **Honour**: solidity, simplicity.
- **Break**: the shield; a closed, interlocking form says "secure" without the icon.

## Universal clichés (any category)

- Swooshes and orbits around a letter.
- A letter inside a circle or a rounded square with no other idea.
- Negative-space arrows (the FedEx move) used for its own sake.
- Overlapping semi-transparent circles in three colours.
- Initials in a shield or crest for a company with no heritage.
- A globe or a lightbulb ("ideas").
- Text set on a circle path around a symbol (outside true emblems).

## What reads as amateur

| Tell | Why it reads amateur |
|---|---|
| Lumpy curves, many nodes, wobbly outlines | Hand-placed points; no construction |
| Shapes built from primitives (rect + circle) | No drawn curve; reads "basic" |
| Filters faking texture (displacement, turbulence, goo blur) | Messy, not a vector, dies small |
| Gloss, bevels, drop shadows, lens flares | 2008 web-button effects rescuing a weak form |
| Gradient + sheen | "Cheap button" |
| Stock font at default spacing | No design decision in the type |
| Hairlines and tiny details | Vanish at 32px; look fragile big |
| Near-miss angles and near touches | Look like mistakes because they are |
| Uneven stroke weights with no system | Inconsistent hand |
| Three or more colours with no reason | No restraint |
| A tagline, URL or "Est. 2024" inside the mark | Clutter; every lockup gets crowded |
| Mark and name in different worlds (rounded cartoon + sharp serif) | No shared DNA |
| Clip-art literalness | The first idea anyone would have |
| Thin parallel drips/tails | Read as legs or tentacles |
| Mark floating off the type's lines in a lockup | Not aligned to measured metrics |
| Different marks at every size with no family resemblance | Not a system |

## Using this file

- Put the category's clichés into the brief block for the concept agents as "forbidden".
- When cutting concepts, any concept whose sentence contains a cliché noun is cut unless it does something
  genuinely new with it, and you can say what in one sentence.
- In `/drip:adapt` packs, the same four lists exist per niche (`drip_guide adapt/pack-skeleton`).
