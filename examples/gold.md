# Gold: shipped pages at the bar

Real first screens from shipped sites, one per kind of page, with what makes each work. Read the one closest to your page
before making comps (open the image with the Read tool). The critic gets the matching one as the bar.

They are a bar for craft, confidence and restraint, never a look to copy: take the decisions, not the surface.

## hardware: a physical product (teenage.engineering, OP-1 product page)
`examples/gold/hardware/1.jpg`, `examples/gold/hardware/2.jpg`

- The product is the whole first screen: one real object, photographed straight on, at a size where every key reads.
- The headline is small and quiet ("the beauty of evolution."), lower case, centred above the object: the object shouts, the type doesn't.
- One neutral ground (warm grey), no gradients, no decoration; the only colour is on the product itself.
- The second screen is another single object (the case) with one line of text and one link: the rhythm is object, line, object.
- What to take: let a real product carry the page, keep the type calm, give each screen one thing. If there is no photo yet, leave a
  labelled slot at this size rather than filling it with a drawing that reads as clip art.

## saas: a software product (linear.app)
`examples/gold/saas/1.jpg`

- A two-line headline (about 64px) on the left, one short sentence under it, then the real product UI filling the lower half
  of the first screen at full fidelity: real issue titles, real panels, a real agent card.
- Near-black ground with one restrained light; the UI itself is the picture, cropped by the screen edge so it reads as bigger than the window.
- No buttons in the hero beyond the nav's sign up: the product screenshot is the call to action.
- What to take: show the real product at full size and let it crop; one headline, one sentence; no decoration that the product doesn't need.

## ai-tool: an AI creative tool (krea.ai)
`examples/gold/ai-tool/1.jpg`

- A centred two-line claim (about 60px) with one line of what it does, two actions (one filled, one quiet).
- The picture is the product in use on a real desk at dusk: a monitor showing Krea's interface, so the claim, the tool and the
  output are one image. No floating cards, no glowing orbs.
- Dark, photographic, warm light from the scene itself.
- What to take: put the tool in a real scene; one claim, two actions; let a photograph carry the mood rather than a gradient.

## studio: a design studio / portfolio (locomotive.ca)
`examples/gold/studio/1.jpg`

- A full-bleed moving picture (a deep red blur of work in motion) with the name set large and light in the bottom left.
- Nav reduced to four words and a contact link in the top corners.
- One custom display face with character (a light, high-contrast serif-like cut), nothing else on the screen.
- What to take: the work and the name are the page; a single confident type move; no taglines, no cards, no counts.

## How to use
- Before board_directions: Read the gold image closest to your page and write one line on what your best direction takes from
  its decisions (not its colours or faces).
- The critic compares your comps and build with it: "would this sit next to the gold page without looking like a template?"

## The polished hero patterns (the target for most briefs)
What the heroes people save on Dribbble have in common: a calm centred or left headline (about 48-72px, a refined face),
one short sentence, one or two pill buttons, a small quiet nav, and ONE rich picture doing the work, with soft light and depth.
Pick the pattern that shows the product best; make it beautifully, not strangely.

- **Glowing product mockup:** near-black ground, a soft coloured radial light (blue, teal, warm; never purple-pink) rising behind
  the real product dashboard, which sits below the headline in a rounded device frame and bleeds off the bottom; a logo strip between.
- **Fan or arc of cards:** white or soft ground, real outputs (photos, renders, results) on rounded tilted cards in an arc around
  a centred serif or grotesk headline; three short benefit columns under the button. Ideal for any tool that makes pictures.
- **Character in a scene:** an illustrated or 3D mascot and a framed scene (a window, a screen) with the headline inside it;
  a soft dark frame around, one brand accent on the button and one word.
- **Product render on dark:** one product (a 3D render or photo) floating centre, lit from the side, a huge faint wordmark behind
  it as texture, headline and button bottom left, a small detail card bottom right.

Each one: the picture is real or rendered for this product, the type stays calm, the light comes from the picture, rounded
corners and crisp 1px borders agree, and the hero has an entrance (the cards fan in, the light rises, the render settles).
